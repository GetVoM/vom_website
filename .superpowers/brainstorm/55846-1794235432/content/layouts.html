Terms and conditions: three ways to lay it out

All three use the real Arabic text and the same reading type (Effra 16px, tall line height, a ~65-character line). What changes is how someone finds the one clause they came for. Click the one that feels right. Each clause gets a number (2.8, 6.7…) so support can send a link straight to it.

A

Contents rail my pick

The classic legal-document layout. On desktop a quiet contents list sits beside the text and follows you as you scroll, marking where you are. On a phone it becomes a thin bar under the header that shows the current section and opens the full list. Nothing else on the page.

B

Most looked-up first

One centred column, no sidebar. Under the title, a row of links to the clauses people ask about most (refunds, training, uptime, support hours, VoM Pay fees), each one jumping to and highlighting the real clause. They point to the clause; they don't summarise it, so nothing legal gets reworded. Then a compact contents box, then the text. A small "contents" button floats while you scroll.

C

Two parts: subscription and VoM Pay

VoM Pay's merchant terms are about three-quarters of the text but only matter to merchants taking payments. This splits the page into two clearly labelled parts on the same page (still one URL, still searchable with Ctrl+F): Part 1 for every subscriber, Part 2 for VoM Pay. The contents rail is grouped by part, and long lists such as the prohibited activities are set in compact columns.
